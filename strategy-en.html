Part 2
The philosophy
One single core idea, and everything flows from it.

My strategy rests on one principle: hunt down an entire crowd stuck on the wrong side of the market, then position myself in the direction they will be forced to unwind.

When too many traders bet in the same direction (for example a mass of sellers/shorts), they become fuel. If they're wrong, they all have to buy back in a panic at the same moment: that creates a violent acceleration, a squeeze. My job isn't to guess, it's to read four signals which, together, say that a crowd is trapped and that a precise target is waiting for it.

The key point that changed with experience: I enter early, as soon as this profile is in place, without waiting for the explosion. The final liquidation cascade isn't my entry signal, it's my exit signal: it's what propels the price all the way to my target. Waiting for it to enter would mean buying the top.

Part 3
The 4 pillars
A real signal ticks four boxes. If even one is missing, I sit it out.
🔥
Pillar 1 · The fuel
The crowd on the wrong side

The crowd AND the big players are on the wrong side (a mass of shorts when I want to buy). They're the ones who will have to buy back. Extreme funding is a clue to this excess, but on its own it often lies.

📈
Pillar 2 · The referee of direction
The real flow (CVD)

CVD tells you who's really in control: buyers or sellers. Buyer CVD = the buyers are there, a true long. Without this flow in my direction, the rest is worthless.

📊
Pillar 3 · The accumulation
Fresh money (OI)

Open Interest has to rise: new money is coming in, the trap is loading. Flat or melting OI = the crowd is quietly unwinding, no squeeze.

🎯
Pillar 4 · The target
The ≥3R cluster

A clear pile of liquidations, at least 3 times further than my risk. That's the price magnet. No real target = no trade, I never invent an objective out of thin air.

📍 Why I enter early
When the 4 boxes are ticked, the right moment is now, not after the explosion. I accept being wrong often (my win rate is below 50%). My edge isn't being right, it's that my wins aim for at least 3 times my losses.
One single target hit pays back several small stops.

Setup classification
Not all setups are created equal. I grade them on three levels: the higher the grade, the more of the 4 boxes are ticked and the clearer the target.
A+
The perfect alignment

All 4 boxes ticked and a clear target at 3R or more. The holy grail: a massively trapped crowd, strong flow, exploding OI, a nearby cluster. Rare and lucrative.

A
The core of the strategy

All 4 boxes solid, target present, one notch below A+. My bread and butter, and by far the most profitable. This is where I trade most often.

B
The incomplete setup

One weak box: mixed flow (CVD), hesitant OI or a far-away target. The riskiest, often a loser. When I can, I stay out.

📌 Remember: the difference between A and B is NOT the trapped crowd, which is often identical. The difference is the number of boxes truly ticked and the quality of the target. A setup where the flow doesn't follow, or whose cluster is too far away, stays a B.

Part 5
Risk management
If you only remember one part of this document, make it this one.

What blows up an account is never one single bad idea, it's bad risk management repeated over and over. The good news: it's the only part of trading you control 100%.

💰
The "1R" principle

On every trade, I risk a fixed amount: 1R = about 1% of the capital. No matter how confident I am, I never risk more. I think in R, not in dollars: it takes the emotion out of the amount.

🛑
The stop loss: sacred

My stop is set before I enter, and I never touch it to move it further away. The day you move your stop back "just this once" is the beginning of the end.

📈
The dynamic Stop Profit

Once the trade is moving in the right direction, I gradually raise my stop under each new low (long) or above each new high (short) to lock in gains.

⚠️ The lesson from trade #14: one of my biggest wins (+5.43R) comes from a sizing error: wrong unit entered on mobile (USDT instead of tokens) → oversized position. I got lucky that the price went my way. If it had turned, that was -5R on a single trade. Never replicate this: the fixed 1R stays sacred, even when the mistake pays off. A positive result never validates a risk management error.

Part 6
Key lessons
Beyond the individual results, which patterns stand out after 92 trades?
🥇 The crowd, not the funding

THE major finding. The real fuel of a squeeze is an entire crowd stuck on the wrong side, not extreme funding. Funding alone often lies: on TNSR, it was at -0.82% (shorts supposedly trapped), but the sellers were right, a clean loss. It's the crowd AND the big players on the wrong side that force the buyback. Pillar #1, non-negotiable.

🥈 BTC is the boss of altcoins

Several trades failed not because of a bad setup, but because Bitcoin made a brutal move at the same moment. On low-caps, the impact is immediate. → Always check the BTC context before entering.

🥉 Stop placement is an art

A stop sitting in an obvious liquidity zone gets hunted, even when the thesis is right: on VELVET, the price grabbed the longs' stops RIGHT before exploding toward my target. → Place stops one notch beyond the "obvious" zones.

🏅 Manage on the right timeframe

Trade #13: analysis and entry on M5, but management on M1 → too much noise, premature exit at +0.24R while the move kept going. → Keep management on the entry timeframe (M5), not below.

💎 I enter early, the ignition is for exiting, not entering. When the 4 boxes are ticked, I enter on the setup as it's forming: waiting for the explosion (the liquidation cascade) means buying the top. That cascade is my EXIT signal, at the first cluster. The price reverses at the 1st cluster (APE, VELVET, RE), not the far one: I aim for 3R there, not some objective made up out of thin air.
